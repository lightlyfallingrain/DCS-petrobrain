### Debug Report

### Observed Issue

Card row 1 ("take into a held heading", off→m1, steady ~200 km/h level). User: "Fail - still
creates bank and escalates out of control." Take at t=112.19, release at t=137.11.

**First 8 s after the take, before any bank oscillation:** the slip ball (arg 943, right positive,
2026-11-20 calibration) runs monotonically −0.03 → +0.56, sideslip −1.9° → +18°, lateral
acceleration `a_r` 0 → +2.5 m/s², yaw rate ≈ 0 (the flight path curves without the nose following —
pure sideslip, not a turn), vertical speed 1.1 → 5.7 m/s. Only after the ball has run away does a
growing ~10 s lateral/vertical oscillation begin (`a_r` +2.5 → −5.3 → +6.2 → −10 m/s², roll rate
peaking 30–37°/s). **Mid-task, the user supplied a direct in-flight observation**: after the take
they saw "lots of left pedal" — i.e. the law was commanding increasing left pedal while the ball
ran right.

### Hypothesis

**Root cause: the pedal/ball-centring loop's sign is backward.** LV-1's section F
(`flight_control.lua`'s `law_update`, "pedals fly the slip ball, always") computes
`d_t_y = k_ball_pedal * (ball - ball_centre)`. `k_ball_pedal`, the collective→pedal torque
feed-forward (`k_pedal_from_collective_rate * u_c_rate * rotor_dir`), and the turn-coordination
nudge (`k_turn_coord_pedal * r_d`) were all signed in `flight_profile.lua` under the assumption
"positive pedal command = right" — stated explicitly in the profile's own pre-fix comment
("`rotor_dir = +1`" for "more collective → more right pedal") and in
`aircraft-layer/research/2026-11-20-slip-ball-recon.md`'s derivation ("With positive pedal command
= right … the ball-centring term is `pedal_dot = +K_beta*(ball-ball_centre)`"). That assumption was
never independently measured; it rested on a weak, confounded correlation
(`2026-11-18-bl16-m0-flight-axes-findings.md`, flight 1, A2–A4: "pedal arg 1103: positive
correlates with positive yaw rate … +0.23..+0.40, which is weak") from hands-on-cyclic cruise
flight with no isolated pedal input, chained through the solidly-measured `SIGN.pedals = -1.0`
command↔arg relation (`command = -arg`, confirmed by a direct write test on two separate flights).

LV-1 is the **first** time this gain was ever flown — LV-0 had no `k_ball_pedal` term at all
(`2026-11-20-slip-ball-recon.md`: "the law's own `K_beta` term … was never implemented"). The
sortie exposed the weak assumption as wrong.

### Evidence

1. **Direct physical fact (solid, not weak)**, 2026-11-20 calibration flight: left pedal → ball
   moves right; right pedal → ball moves left ("step on the ball" needs *right* pedal to centre a
   ball that is right of centre).
2. **This sortie's own direct observation**: "lots of left pedal" while `ball` ran from −0.03 to
   +0.56 (right). Combining with (1): the law was increasing left pedal *while the ball was already
   right* — i.e. `d_t_y > 0` (more left pedal, per the pre-fix convention) coincided with `ball >
   ball_centre`, exactly what `d_t_y = +k_ball_pedal*(ball-ball_centre)` with `k_ball_pedal > 0`
   produces. Left pedal then pushes the ball further right (1), closing a positive-feedback loop:
   this is decisive, not inferred from the weak correlation — **positive pedal command is
   physically LEFT, not right.**
3. **Shape of the trace matches positive feedback, not a one-off bias or a different loop.** The
   ball grows smoothly and monotonically for 8 s with yaw rate ≈ 0 (not a turn, not a heading-hold
   fight) before any bank/roll oscillation appears — consistent with a slowly-building sideslip
   from a runaway integrator (`t_y` is a position/integrator, not a direct proportional output:
   `next_state.t_y = state.t_y + d_t_y*dt`), not with a sign error in the roll/cyclic channel (which
   would show as a steady bias or an immediate, not delayed, bank divergence). The bank oscillation
   that follows is consistent with the real aircraft's own roll response to a growing, uncommanded
   sideslip (dihedral-type coupling) feeding back into the lift-vector law's own trim loop
   (`e_r = a_r_d - a_r`, with `a_r_d ≈ 0` since no turn is commanded but `a_r` growing from the real
   sideslip) — a secondary, correctly-structured response to a primary disturbance the pedal loop
   itself created, not a second independent bug.

### Which term dominated

Three pedal terms exist; only one drove the initial divergence:

- **`k_ball_pedal` (0.3, now −0.3) — dominant.** It is the only term that is large, continuously
  growing, and a direct function of the ball error alone. With `ball` climbing from −0.03 to +0.56
  over 8 s, `d_t_y` (pre-fix sign) grows from ≈ −0.02 to ≈ +0.16 over that window and is *integrated*
  into `t_y` every `law_hz` (30 Hz) step — a steadily increasing left-pedal command, matching both
  the user's direct observation and the ball's own smooth, monotonic growth shape exactly.
- **`k_pedal_from_collective_rate` (0.25) * `rotor_dir` — secondary, smaller.** Only active while
  `measured.collective_enabled` and only proportional to the *rate* of collective change
  (`u_c_rate`), not a position error — bounded by `k_collective` (0.08) times a modest
  vertical-acceleration error during a gentle climb-out (`v_u` 1.1 → 5.7 m/s over 8 s is a mild
  ~0.6 m/s² average, not a sharp step). Order-of-magnitude estimate from the gains:
  `d_t_y` contribution on the order of 0.01–0.03, roughly 5–15% of the ball term's contribution in
  the same window — present and (sharing the same wrong-convention assumption) likely compounding
  in the same direction, but not large enough on its own to explain the shape or rate of the
  observed divergence. **Not directly measured this flight** — see "What remains unproven" below;
  the new trace fields (`u_c_rate`, `u_c`) will make this exact next time.
- **`k_turn_coord_pedal` (0.02) * `r_d` — negligible in the critical window.** Only non-zero once
  `|r_d| >= turn_settle_deg_s` (0.3°/s), and the feedback's own note records `psidot ≈ 0` for the
  first 8 s (no turn is being commanded or flown) — `r_d` stays near zero through the window that
  matters, so this term contributes essentially nothing until well after the ball loop has already
  run away and the aircraft is genuinely yawing.

### 2026-11-23 "integrator windup" diagnosis — checked, a different bug

`plans/flight-lift-vector/debug-2026-11-23.md`, problem 3, diagnosed an erratic take (off→m1 and
m2→m1, "bank goes to 60+ degrees … spirals out of control") as an under-damped/unguarded `phi_i`
integrator (Decision 30's named risk) and the `q`/`r = -ang.y` body-rate-vs-Euler-rate confusion —
closed by LV-1's section H anti-windup and the Euler-rate damping in `law_outputs`. That diagnosis
is **not the same bug and was not wrong for its own flight**: it was taken against the pre-LV-1
law, which had **no `k_ball_pedal` term at all** (the slip-ball recon note is explicit that LV-0
never implemented it). A loop that does not exist cannot have a sign error, so the 2026-11-23
divergence and this one are two separate root causes in two different code states — the earlier fix
(anti-windup, section H) is unrelated to and does not touch the pedal/ball loop this pass fixes.

### Why the existing tests did not catch this

`aircraft-layer/tests/test_flight_control_law.py`'s `_measured()` helper (the default `measured`
table every scenario in the file starts from) **has no `ball` key at all**. `law_update`'s own
`local ball = measured.ball or profile.ball_centre` therefore falls back to `profile.ball_centre`
in every single pre-existing scenario — `(ball - ball_centre)` is identically `0.0` everywhere,
so `k_ball_pedal`'s sign (and magnitude) was never exercised by any test, not even indirectly. This
is not a test plant sharing the law's wrong assumption (the usual way a sign bug slips through) —
the term was simply never driven with a non-centred ball at all. `TestTurnCoordinationPedal`
exercises `k_turn_coord_pedal`'s sign (against the same wrong "positive = right" convention, now
corrected) but nothing exercised `k_ball_pedal` or `k_pedal_from_collective_rate`.

### Fix Applied

`aircraft-layer/dcs-export/Petrobrain/flight_profile.lua`: flipped the three Section F gains that
were signed under "positive pedal command = right" —

- `k_ball_pedal`: `0.3` → `-0.3`
- `rotor_dir`: `1.0` → `-1.0` (flips `k_pedal_from_collective_rate`'s effective sign; the
  magnitude/physical-coupling meaning is unchanged — "more collective needs more right pedal" is
  still true, only which command sign *is* right pedal changes)
- `k_turn_coord_pedal`: `0.02` → `-0.02`
- `pedal_limit_positive`/`pedal_limit_negative`: swapped magnitudes (`0.5`/`-1.0` → `1.0`/`-0.5`) —
  the tight 0.5 bound was placed on the command-positive side because "right pedal is the scarce
  side" was assumed to be positive; it is now negative, so the bound moves with it.

`SIGN.pedals = -1.0` in `flight_control.lua` (the measured `command = -arg` relation, confirmed by
a direct write test on two separate flights, M0-B) is **untouched** — it was never in question;
only the separate, never-measured "which command sign is physically right pedal" assumption was
wrong. The `law_update` formula itself (Section F) is unchanged; only the three gains' signs and
the two limit magnitudes moved, in `flight_profile.lua` and its test-file mirrors.

**Instrumentation added** (testing aid, same flag/file as the existing flight trace): the trace
previously carried no law internals at all. `format_trace_line` now also takes the active `fc_law`
state, `fc_mode`, and `fc_last_command`, and prints `mode`, `phi_d`/`bank`, `theta_d`/`pitch`,
`t_r`/`t_p`/`t_y`, `turn_coord_pedal`, `u_c`, `phi_i`/`theta_i`, and the written axis values. `r_d`
and `a_f_d`/`a_r_d`/`a_u_d`/`u_c_rate` are locals inside `law_update` with nowhere to live between
frames, so four diagnostic-only fields (`dbg_r_d`, `dbg_a_f_d`, `dbg_a_r_d`, `dbg_a_u_d`,
`dbg_u_c_rate`) were added to the law's own state table, written at the point each value is
computed, zeroed at take, and — like the existing `fc_debug_estimator_sample_count` — never read by
any `law_*`/`est_*` function and never sent on any wire.

### What remains unproven

- **`rotor_dir`'s own physical direction** ("more collective needs more right pedal on the
  Mi-24P") is a stated physical assumption, not a measured one — only its *command-domain sign*
  was corrected here to match the newly-established "positive = left" fact; the underlying claim
  that more collective needs more right pedal at all has never been isolated by a flight. One
  observation that would prove or disprove it: hold heading and ball centred, make a deliberate
  collective step with pedals held fixed, and read which way the ball moves.
- **The collective FF term's actual contribution this flight is estimated, not measured** — the
  trace carried no `u_c`/`u_c_rate` field at the time of the sortie (fixed by this pass's own
  instrumentation), so the "5–15% of the ball term" figure above is an order-of-magnitude estimate
  from the gains and the measured climb rate, not a direct reading. The next flight's trace will
  show `u_c_rate`/`u_c` directly.
- **Whether the fixed sign fully resolves the reported "escalates out of control"**, or whether the
  secondary bank/roll oscillation (the ~10 s period, 30–37°/s roll-rate peaks) has an independent
  contributor once the primary pedal runaway is removed — not flown yet. The new trace fields
  (`phi_i`, `t_r`, `turn_coord_pedal`) are exactly what the next flight needs to settle this if the
  oscillation recurs in a different shape.
- **A standing safety guard** (ball beyond a bound and still growing, or bank beyond a bound → hand
  back with a spoken reason, per the existing handback path) was considered but not built — it is a
  behaviour change, not a bug fix, and the task's instruction was to propose rather than build it
  unless trivial. Given this is the second pedal-sign-adjacent divergence in two sorties (though a
  different mechanism each time — 11-23's unguarded `phi_i`, now this), a bound on `|ball -
  ball_centre|` growing past, say, 2–3× the usable range (profile's own `ball_usable_range = 0.5`)
  while the rate of growth stays positive for several seconds, triggering the existing release/
  handback path with a spoken cause, seems like a reasonable next guard — but it is a plan-level
  decision (what counts as "still growing", what the spoken cause should say), not something to add
  silently in a debug pass.

## Fix round, 2026-11-24 (review required fix: the collective-near-pedal-limit guard)

Reviewed in `review-debug-1124.md` (`844213f1` + `d0b4d6cc`): the pedal-sign fix itself was sound,
but `law_update`'s collective-near-pedal-limit guard — the `if u_c_rate > 0.0 then if state.t_y
>= (profile.pedal_limit_positive - profile.pedal_near_limit_margin) then ... end` block, just above
Section F — was not updated to match the new sign convention, and had zero test coverage either
side of the original fix (the review's own grep across both test files confirmed this). Before the
fix it correctly guarded the side `k_pedal_from_collective_rate * rotor_dir` actually pushed `t_y`
toward (`rotor_dir = +1`, `pedal_limit_positive`); after it, `rotor_dir = -1` flips that push
negative, but the guard still hard-coded `pedal_limit_positive` — so it silently stopped protecting
the scarce right-pedal limit it exists to protect, while instead engaging on the abundant,
never-at-risk side. Confirmed by the review's own direct mutation (`t_y = -0.42` → uncapped;
`t_y = 0.92` → capped, backwards from the guard's job) and reproduced independently here before
fixing (see below).

**Fix**: `flight_control.lua` gains one small helper, `law_sign(v)` (next to `law_clamp`), and the
guard now derives which limit it is protecting from `law_sign(profile.k_pedal_from_collective_rate
* profile.rotor_dir)` — the same coefficient whose sign Section F's own ball-centring formula
already reads at line 964 — rather than naming `pedal_limit_positive` directly. A positive sign
checks proximity to `pedal_limit_positive` (as before); a negative sign (the post-fix convention)
checks `pedal_limit_negative + pedal_near_limit_margin` instead. A future sign correction to either
factor now moves this guard with it instead of stranding it again. Every other pedal-domain
consumer was re-grepped (`t_y`, `pedal`, `rotor_dir`, `pedal_limit`, `pedal_near`,
`SIGN.pedals`) and confirmed unaffected: `SIGN.pedals` (never in question), `law_outputs`'s pedal
output and the anti-windup clamp at line 966 (both already symmetric over both limits), and
`ai_pilot_wheel.lua`'s unrelated park-detection read.

**New regression coverage**, `test_flight_control_law.py::TestCollectiveRateCapNearPedalLimit` —
two toy-plant cases against the real law (same `h_m`/`alt_goal_m` as
`TestRotorEnvelopeGuards.test_well_clear_of_both_guards_collective_rises_freely`, `w_mps` widened
to `-10.0` only so this scenario's own nominal, uncapped rate (0.447, by direct computation) clears
`collective_rate_cap_near_pedal_limit` (0.3) and a reading at or below it is only possible because
the guard engaged): `t_y = -0.42` (within margin of the new scarce `pedal_limit_negative`) must cap
to `0.3`; `t_y = 0.92` (near the new abundant `pedal_limit_positive`) must not cap at all. **Both
confirmed red under the pre-fix guard** (reverted the guard to its pre-fix form, the two new tests
failed exactly as the review's own mutation predicted — `t_y = -0.42` read uncapped, `t_y = 0.92`
read capped — then the fix was restored).

**Optional items also done**: `flight_profile.lua`'s `rotor_dir` comment now points to this
report's own "What remains unproven" section rather than stating the physical claim as flat
narrative; `body-layer/ROADMAP/BL-16.md`'s pre-fix "Open" bullet gets a dated
`**Superseded 2026-11-24**` correction (not deleted) recording that "positive pedal command =
