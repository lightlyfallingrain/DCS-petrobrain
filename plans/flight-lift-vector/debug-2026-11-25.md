### Debug Report

### Observed Issue

BL-16 LV-1 speed changes (150 → 250 km/h and the mirror) are still far too slow after the fifth
re-fly, despite the fourth re-fly's amendment having already raised `c_theta` (0.5 → 0.85) and
`speed_accel_cap_mps2` (0.5 → 1.9). User, verbatim: *"pitch changes more now, but not enough and
above all it takes much too long to reach the pitch."* Expected: pitch/collective reach the
acceleration cap within ~1–2 s, hold it through the cruise, then settle smoothly over ~5 s near
the target (`docs/acceptance/2026-11-24-sortie-feedback.md`, fourth re-fly's own description).
Measured (fifth re-fly, `logs-2026-11-25/refly5_flight_trace.log`): 160→250 km/h took ~50 s,
255→~150 took ~40+ s; wanted acceleration peaked at only 1.6 m/s² (never the 1.9 cap) 15 s after
the command, and the delivered acceleration at that peak was only ~1.1 m/s² against ~9.6° pitch.

Mid-task, two scope additions arrived from the user (relayed via the coordinator), both folded into
this same pass since they sit in the same speed-change path:

1. An RPM calibration flight (`docs/acceptance/2026-11-24-sortie-feedback.md`, merged `aea1690e`):
   gauge % = arg × 110 (not × 100) for both engine (args 40/41, 838/839) and rotor (42/845),
   confirmed within 1.1% at three reference states — fits all six readings closely: level 170 km/h
   (engine 0.830/0.832 → 92%, rotor 0.872 → 97%), fast acceleration (engine 0.900/0.887 → 99%/98%,
   rotor 0.811 → 90%), full-down descent (engine 0.665/0.666 → 74%, rotor 0.915 → 101%).
2. A rotor-RPM warning spec (merged `f8a8f93a`): the real Mi-24 gives the pilot an audio warning as
   rotor RPM nears the limit, cueing them to ease the collective; Petrovich has no audio, so his
   stand-in is the rotor gauge itself. From a warning level (first cut 88%, tunable) he should stop
   adding collective and actively ease it, so rotor never reaches the 85% generator-trip floor.
   Rotor RPM only, not engine RPM (the already-designed anticipatory schedule owns that).

### Hypothesis

**Speed slowness.** The speed shaper (`flight_control.lua`, "Step 1 (speed)") is a
critically-damped second-order reference generator with bandwidth `omega_speed`, shipped at
0.15 rad/s. For a 100 km/h (~27.8 m/s) step, this filter's own acceleration demand never approaches
`speed_accel_cap_mps2` (1.9): it peaks around 0.6–1.6 m/s² and settles over several shaper time
constants (~4–5/ω ≈ 27–33 s). The fourth re-fly's raised `c_theta`/`speed_accel_cap_mps2` were never
reached because the shaper feeding them was the real bottleneck, not the pitch/collective channels
underneath. Pitch under-delivery (wanted 1.6, delivered 1.1 m/s² at −9°) is a secondary symptom of
the same root cause: the `k_speed_error` feedback term (which pushes pitch past the pure kinematic
tilt exactly to compensate for real-aircraft drag) never grows large because the shaper's own `v_ref`
stays close to the real, lagging aircraft when both are this slow.

**RPM.** Confirmed directly from the merged calibration data (not a hypothesis): the shipped
×100 scale under-read both gauges; arg × 110 fits. The engine-RPM-gated collective schedule and
rotor-RPM floor guard were both shipped `"dummy"` pending exactly this flight (`U9`).

**Rotor-RPM warning.** New behaviour per explicit user spec, not a bug — the existing margin-based
freeze-only guard (`rotor_rpm_near_floor_margin_pct`/`collective_rate_cap_near_rpm_floor`) only ever
capped a *further increase* at zero; it never reduced collective, so it could not recover lost RPM,
only stop losing more. The user's own spec (audio-warning analogy) explicitly asks for an active
ease, which needs a new, unconditionally-applied mechanism rather than widening the old one.

### Evidence

- `flight_control.lua`'s "Step 1 (speed)" formula read and matched against the fifth re-fly trace's
  own columns (`a_f_d`/`theta_d` vs `t`): peak `a_f_d` 1.60 at t≈286 (15 s after the t≈271.4
  command), never 1.9; consistent with a critically-damped filter whose own peak-then-decay shape
  is set by `omega_speed`, not the downstream caps.
- Toy-plant harness (ad hoc, built on the committed `TestSpeedChannelIsBrisk`/`TestAmendmentProductionValuesTogether`
  pattern — same `law_init_at_take`/`law_apply_goal`/`law_update`/`law_outputs` pure functions, no
  DCS mock needed): raising `omega_speed` alone (no other change) reproduces overshoot/hunting once
  the shaper's own acceleration demand saturates `speed_accel_cap_mps2` for an extended stretch —
  classic reference-generator windup once amplitude-saturated. Confirmed by sweeping `omega_speed`
  0.3→1.0 with the *existing* `max_accel_mps2` clamp only: sign changes (oscillation count) jump
  from 1 to 7 past ~0.6–0.7, overshoot growing to 1–3 m/s.
- Adding a continuous braking-distance ceiling on `dv_ref` (`sqrt(2·speed_accel_cap·|err_v|)`, no
  mode switch, so nothing to chatter across) eliminates that windup at every `omega_speed` tested up
  to 1.0, confirmed by the same harness (sign changes back to 1, overshoot ≤0.07 m/s at
  `omega_speed=0.5`, production `max_accel_mps2=2.0`).
- Re-ran the same sweep with a more realistic pitch→acceleration gain (0.12/deg, derived from the
  fifth re-fly's own delivered 1.1 m/s² at −9°, vs the toy suite's 2.0/deg): same qualitative result
  — smooth, single-peak-then-decay, and `theta_d` reaches a *larger* peak (−12.6° at `omega_speed=0.4`,
  −14.3° at 0.5) than the pure kinematic feed-forward alone would give, because `v_ref` now outruns
  the lagging real aircraft sooner, which is exactly the `k_speed_error` mechanism closing the drag
  deficit the fifth re-fly flagged — no separate fix needed for that complaint.
- `theta_d = theta_i + delta_theta_ff` was unclamped as a sum (`theta_i` alone was bounded to
  [−15, 15]; `delta_theta_ff`, the feed-forward, was not) — confirmed by reading `law_update`
  section D. With `k_speed_error`'s push now routinely larger, this is a real, not theoretical, path
  past −15° (`delta_theta_ff` alone reaches −12.6° at `omega_speed=0.4` in the realistic-gain run
  above, before `theta_i` adds anything).
- Full `aircraft-layer` suite (1176→1177 tests as fixtures were added/updated) green before and
  after every commit in this pass (`pytest aircraft-layer/tests -q`).
- RPM calibration and the rotor-RPM-warning toy-plant verification: see "Verification" below.

### Fix Applied

1. **`flight_profile.lua`: `omega_speed` 0.15 → 0.5.** The speed shaper now saturates
   `speed_accel_cap_mps2` within ~1–2 s and holds it through the cruise, matching the user's own
   description of the shape.
2. **`flight_control.lua`, "Step 1 (speed)": added a continuous braking-distance ceiling on
   `dv_ref`** (`dv_cap = min(limits.max_accel_mps2, sqrt(2·speed_accel_cap·|err_v|))`) — the
   anti-windup that makes raising `omega_speed` safe. No new profile constant; derived purely from
   existing ones.
3. **`flight_control.lua`, section D: clamp the combined `theta_d`** (`theta_i + delta_theta_ff`) to
   `[theta_min_deg, theta_max_deg]`, not just `theta_i` alone — keeps "−15° is about the max" true
   under the now-larger `k_speed_error` push, rather than assuming it falls out of the two bounded
   parts on their own.
4. **RPM gauge scale, both `ROTOR_RPM_ARG_SCALE_MAX_PCT` and `ENGINE_RPM_ARG_SCALE_MAX_PCT`:
   100.0 → 110.0.** `ROTOR_RPM_SOURCE`/`ENGINE_RPM_SOURCE` flipped `"dummy"` → `"arg_proxy"` — both
   now read live. This turns on the engine-RPM-gated collective-rate schedule and (see next point)
   the rotor floor protection.
5. **New rotor-RPM warning (`flight_profile.lua`: `rotor_rpm_warning_pct = 88.0`,
   `collective_ease_rate_near_rpm_warning = -0.3`), replacing the old margin-based freeze guard
   outright.** In `flight_control.lua`, below the warning threshold `u_c_rate` is forced to at most
   the (negative) ease rate, applied *unconditionally* — not gated behind "rate wants to increase"
   the way the superseded guard was, since an ease is itself a decrease. Engine-RPM schedule is
   untouched and stays independent (rotor here, engine there, per the user's explicit "not engine
   RPM for this").

  calibrated, engine/rotor coupling model — first-order engine spool toward a collective-proportional
  demand, rotor droop proportional to the engine's own shortfall; constants picked only to produce a
  plausible droop-and-recover shape of the right order of magnitude, since the one real data point
  — collective 0.13→full in ~3 s, rotor to 85.4% — is a control-response curve's single sample, not
  a fit). A climb+speed step that drives collective hard:
  - **Engine-RPM schedule alone** (rotor warning disabled): min rotor **83.3%** — crosses *below*

    97.0–97.3% in that scenario, guard never engaged) — the warning is a climb/aggressive-collective
    case, not an ordinary speed change.

    it verifies the *mechanism* (the ease engages and prevents the crossing a same-droop-model run
    without it does not), not the exact RPM numbers a real flight will show. Owed by the next flight
    with the warning actually engaged.
- Symptom gone: the fifth re-fly's own trace values (peak `a_f_d` 1.6 reached at t+15s, not within
  1–2 s) no longer match the post-fix toy-plant shape, which saturates the cap immediately and holds
  it — consistent with the user's own described shape. No regression found in the climb/descend,
  bank-take, level-take or turn rows (none of those paths were touched).
