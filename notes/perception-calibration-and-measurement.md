# Perception Calibration & Measurement

- **"Well-observed" does not mean "triangulated" — crossing-bearing fusion does not reliably tighten bearing uncertainty the way same-bearing repetition does.** With 2x2-covariance position fusion, repeated looks from *roughly the same* relative bearing preserve a favourable down-range-heavy anisotropy while shrinking toward the systematic-bias floor, and reliably collapse `bearing_uncertainty_deg` to a single sweep step. Looks fused from *crossing* bearings — real triangulation, which shrinks the overall covariance radius the most — smear the tightened covariance's orientation away from any one observer's own cross-range axis, so a query against it can read *worse* along one particular axis than a single, luckily-aligned look would; it does not reliably reach single-step even fully converged. The two scenarios respond differently to the same tuning constants (`LOOK_SWEEP_SIGMA`, `SYSTEMATIC_BIAS_FRACTION`), so "the contact has been seen a lot" is not by itself a predictor of how tight the optic sweep gets — the geometry of *how* it was seen matters as much as how often (precise-position-belief implementation, Stage 5 discovery, verified numerically before the test was written).

- **A mechanical Stage-1 declaration (an uncertainty field) and the Stage-2 behaviour it describes (actually perturbing the number) can ship independently, and only one of them is visible in a diff review that trusts the field's presence.** `perception/hybrid_source.py` got `PositionUncertainty(300, 300)` in Stage 1 but never got Stage 2's `perturbed_bearing_range` call — the channel kept handing belief truth-exact geometry behind a cosmetic band, on the channel the pilot actually flies with, and the plan's own explicit decision point about whether to extend the treatment there was never recorded as answered by either implementer. Caught only because Reviewer traced the call site rather than trusting the presence of the uncertainty field as proof the channel had been perturbed. When an error-model milestone touches multiple emission channels, verify each one calls the perturbation function, not just that each declares an uncertainty struct (precise-position-belief review, required fix).

- **Screenshot angle derivation requires attitude correction from airframe boresight.** When calibrating a body-relative perception mask by reading angles off cockpit screenshots, the visible horizon in the image sits offset from the airframe's boresight by the aircraft's pitch angle. Calibration protocol: measure angles relative to image center (boresight) where possible; where only the horizon is usable as reference, subtract the assumed pitch at capture time to recover boresight-relative angles. Document the assumed pitch alongside the derived table, so future re-derivations from captures at a different attitude can correct consistently rather than inheriting this one's attitude error. Failure to apply this correction produces a systematic offset in every derived breakpoint (cockpit-visibility, plan D7).

- **Correlated fusion between two highly-correlated, similar-magnitude looks can produce a non-positive-semidefinite covariance, and the existing mean-residual sanity guard does not catch it.** Bar-Shalom-Campo correlated fusion (`fold_position`) can yield a negative trace/determinant exactly in the repeated-same-geometry dwell case — a mean that still sits close to one input's own mean, while the covariance itself is degenerate. A mean-residual guard (`FUSION_SANITY_SIGMA`) checks the mean, not the covariance, so it is a different failure surface and misses this one entirely; a second explicit PSD check (`_is_valid_covariance`) is needed as its own trigger for the "hold" branch. Any fusion formula combining highly-correlated inputs needs its covariance output checked for validity directly, not inferred from the mean looking reasonable (relative-distance-grouping implementation, Stage 5, caught by a scenario test, not by inspection).

- **A gate's "hypothesis geometry" (observer-to-believed-position) and the incoming look's own real geometry are not interchangeable correlation inputs, even though using the real geometry looks like the more honest choice.** For a correlated-gate association test to close a specific false-split case (two groups on one line-of-sight bearing merging because of their naive independent-sum width), the correlation term must be computed from the *belief's own anchor* to the new look, not from the new look's real, true position — using the real geometry reproduces the old over-wide gate, because a genuinely different, closer object's real geometry is of course decorrelated from the far anchor. Verified by hand-computing both against the same worked numbers, not assumed from which one sounds more principled (relative-distance-grouping implementation, Stage 5).

- **A random-Fourier-feature field realised once (not resampled per query) needs many more components than the theorem's ensemble-average target suggests, to make one fixed draw's spatial variance match the target.** `_FIELD_FOURIER_K` needed 256 components, not the 16-32 initially planned, because each optic-class error field is one fixed realisation (keyed by name/optic, never redrawn), so what matters is that one draw's own spatial variance, not the RFF ensemble average. 16-32 draws left individual classes' realised variance anywhere from 0.5x-1.5x of target; 256 kept every shipped class within ±10%. When a stochastic field is drawn once and reused for the whole run (rather than resampled), measure the realised variance of the actual shipped draw, not the theorem's asymptotic guarantee (relative-distance-grouping implementation, Stage 5).

- **A correlated gate's hypothesis-range rescaling can amplify an already-loose look's sigma when the two looks are in the opposite optic-tightness order from the one a simpler prototype measured.** `passes_gate`'s hypothesis-scaling multiplies the second-observed look's own declared sigma by `hypothesis_range_m / percept.range_m`; when the second-observed look is the looser optic and that ratio exceeds 1 (far-first order, hypothesis range is the larger, earlier-founded range), the rescale widens `S` and makes the Mahalanobis test *more* permissive — the opposite of what narrowing a gate around a tighter look should do. An architect's simpler prototype measurement that does not include this rescale can report a materially better (lower) worst-case fold rate than the shipped mechanism actually produces; re-measure against the shipped code's own gate, not the prototype's, before quoting a number to the user as a decision input (relative-distance-grouping review round 3, traced via an instrumented `correlated_innovation` call, not guessed).
