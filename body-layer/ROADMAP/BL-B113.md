# BL-B113 — Same-optic re-sighting splits one group ~10% of the time

- [>] **BL-B113 — The same group, re-sighted through the same optic, stays one contact only ~90%
  (naked eye) / ~93% (binoculars) of the time, against the ~99% a 3σ gate should give.** Deferred
  until it shows up in missions. #status/deferred

  User, 2026-11-09: *"stop here. If this becomes a problem in missions, we'll investigate more, but
  not now."*

  **Measured** (`body-layer/tools/measure_same_group_fold.py`, per-trial resampled noise): flat
  across gaps 2–120 s and ranges 1–4.5 km; cross-optic re-sightings fold 98–99%.

  **Known:** the gate's innovation covariance `S` has the right size (trace within ~5% of the
  empirical innovation covariance) but the wrong shape; the empirical covariance itself gives the
  chi-square(2) 1.1%. Ruled out: the field-correlation model (exact at matched geometry) and building
  `C` along reported vs true bearing (true axis calibrates worse). The ill-conditioned-S defect was
  fixed on `fix/same-optic-gate-indefinite` but does not move the rate.
  `body-layer/tools/measure_gate_calibration.py` reproduces the calibration.

  **Untested candidates** (`plans/same-optic-gate-indefinite/debug.md`, "Open item"): a full 2×2
  rather than down/cross comparison; `hypothesis_scale` interacting with the cap; higher moments of
  the Random-Fourier-Feature field.

  **For the pilot:** roughly one naked-eye re-sighting in ten could leave one group as two contacts.
  Reopen if Petrovich is heard reporting the same group twice. Also bears on `[[BL-B110]]` Stage 2
  (a "new group" callout would voice such a split).
