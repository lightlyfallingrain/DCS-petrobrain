Petrobrain Milestones

Six subsystems, two hundred tracked items — and the chain that was parked four days ago on the pilot's own eye is unparked, rebuilt and accepted. The third landform detector (geomorphons, classified then traced) was built with its cache in from the first pass, and the 2026-10-04 full-theatre rebuild cleared it by the pilot's own criteria: zero relief-gate violations, 234,799 ridge and valley lines, a 720 MB store where 2.4 GB was predicted, the Bekaa floor clean and Palmyra's chains intact by eye. Nothing in the cockpit changes from it — the three callout stages are still unbuilt and still gated on a consumer that wants landform references. Everything that would change the cockpit went the other way this cycle: the 10 km bubble, lost contacts out of clustering, a silence command, merge-echo suppression and group lines that stop repeating what was already said. All of it is merged, and none of it has been heard in the air.

What the drawing shows that the lists do not. Nearly everything now hangs off one node, and it is a flight. Eight separate acceptance cards have accumulated on main because feature after feature was merged ahead of its sortie — which was the right call each time, on your own reasoning that intertwined work is better tested against current HEAD, and which has the compounding side effect this picture makes obvious. The board has stopped being a set of parallel chains and become a funnel.

Two fixes landed on top of a rule neither of them touched, and the drawing is where that shows. Merge-echo suppression and the group-disclosure fix both silence a symptom of ContactStore.ingest's “two-or-more candidates → always found a new contact” rule. The rule itself is still there, which is why BL-B24, BL-B25 and the old fragmentation defect all sit in one node downstream of them rather than being closed by them. Both roadmap entries say so explicitly — the value of drawing it is that the three stop reading as unrelated backlog.

The landform group is no longer a chain, it is a question. Detection was the hard part and it is solved and accepted at theatre scale. What is left is not engineering: 234,799 ridge and valley lines exist and almost none of them are worth saying. That is the same question the sixteen-vehicles node asks about contacts, from the opposite direction — which is why the two are now joined, and why neither of them is a coding task.

Waiting on you, not on code

Three. The landform acceptance that sat here last time is cleared, and what replaced it is one large flight, one small isolated one, and a question only you can answer. Nothing here can be settled from the Mac.

  • Sortie
    Eight cards on main, none heard in the air

    Everything merged since 2026-09-25 is waiting on one flight: group reporting and the 12 m line-of-sight tolerance, the confirm band, the 2026-09-26 crew-behaviour fixes, the brain's first real model, the cohesion redesign, silence, and the two fixes that stop the same units being announced twice. The two newest are the reason to fly now rather than later: both reduce what you hear, and both carry the same risk in the same direction — a genuine split immediately after a merge is indistinguishable from a merge echo at the speech layer and also goes quiet. That is an accepted cost on paper; whether it is ever actually noticed is a cockpit question. Fly with --detection-trace: the belief log structurally cannot record a rejected candidate.

  • Your judgement
    What should he say about sixteen vehicles in one sector?

    This is the open question under all of the quieting work, and it is explicitly recorded as needing your judgement before anyone writes a plan. The 2026-09-19 vocabulary decisions do not cover it: they assumed the plural case arrives as one plural contact, not as sixteen separately-resolvable singular ones — and the 2026-09-28 log showed the clustering is right to see them as sixteen. So the remaining lever is what a crew member would actually say, and no amount of code reading produces that sentence.

  • Windows box
    Fly the elevation-cost probe — alone, with nothing else running

    The hook is already deployed on the Windows box and writes straight to dcs.log; no collector, no body layer, no audio. Two things it settles that no log elsewhere can: whether land.getHeight works through the mission bridge at all (high confidence, never once demonstrated), and whether the call stutters the frame — which only you can judge, because milliseconds in a log cannot record a hitch. Fly it separately from any crew-behaviour sortie: a deliberate frame hitch is precisely what would contaminate a judgement about whether callouts feel right. Card: docs/acceptance/2026-09-29-elevation-cost-probe.md.

Open work

Accepted, unconsumed
body-layer
Two fixes that make him quieter, and both can be too quiet

Merge-echo suppression silences a freshly-founded contact's first callout when an existing contact is plausibly the same real thing (17 of 34 foundings on the 2026-10-04 sortie, reconstructed); the group fix silences a group's opening line when every member was already named, speaking only the delta. Both push in the direction you asked for. Both share one accepted cost, named in the plan rather than discovered later: a genuine split right after a merge is indistinguishable from an echo and also goes quiet. Only a flight can say whether that is ever noticed.

Merged, unflown
body-layer
The rule underneath all three noise defects is still untouched

ContactStore.ingest treats “two or more plausible candidates” as ambiguous and founds a new contact rather than guessing — deliberate anti-guessing, and the engine behind the duplication runaway, the fragmenting outpost, and BL-B24's two-contacts-after-a-long-gap case. Everything shipped this cycle muted symptoms one layer up, at the speech boundary, and every one of those roadmap entries says so in its own text. BL-B25 is the small honest footnote: the suppression predicate is re-evaluated each tick rather than snapshotted, so it can forget that it suppressed something.

Diagnosed, deliberate
aircraft-layer
Let DCS answer line of sight, batched, next to the game

The bridge turned out cheap — 568 units, 1.99 ms mean — so one batched sweep can compute line of sight for every unit in the bubble instead of body-layer asking per candidate over the LAN. Terrain and buildings, true position to true position, which is new capability rather than a fix: nothing occludes behind a building today. Not omniscience — line of sight is a physical fact, and belief about position stays as fuzzy as it is now.

Planned
body-layer
Conditions: the planned model cannot work, and the measurements say why

The first real figures from rain and low light break the shape the roadmap assumed. Within one condition the factor ranges 0.04–0.25 depending on which instrument looks, and the wide/narrow ordering reverses between rain and darkness — rain attacks the windscreen, which the eye and binoculars look through and the sight does not; darkness attacks contrast, where magnification does not help. Detection also needs a ceiling, not a multiplier: in rain both sight fields stop at the same 3.7 km.

Reshaped
body-layer
Three cohesion constants, all of them agreed guesses

Twenty unit widths bounds an ordinary group, so the boundary scales with what the units are rather than being a flat distance. A real SAM installation gets its own 500 m cap, grounded in S-75/S-125 emplacement doctrine. Infantry merge with no backstop at all — “ok to merge infantry too eagerly” — which also bridges two non-infantry vehicles into one group through an infantry member between them. Every one of those wants ears, not another review round.

Needs a flight
brain-layer
follow that tank still cannot pick the right contact

The validator requires the model's evidence to appear verbatim in the candidate it picked — which is the right rule, and currently unsatisfiable: the candidate summaries carry no place names and no class/type structure, so nothing the pilot says can be a literal substring of them. The fix is to give candidates structure, not to loosen the rule.

The double-line edges are DCS itself — read and written, never a one-way feed.
