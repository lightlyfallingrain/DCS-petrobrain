Petrobrain Milestones

Six subsystems, three hundred and ten tracked items — and the attack run flew. Twice, on 2026-10-15: the first thing Petrovich does that helps you attack rather than understand. It worked structurally and sounded wrong in four measurable ways — 38 % of guidance utterances were dead repeats and 68 % of “obstructed” calls were rear-aspect, Petrovich talking at a target behind the airframe. All four are fixed and merged. The sharpest finding is the one that could not be heard: the register that word belongs to has no live caller at all. A contact can only reach belief if its line of sight was clear, so the flag the attack run reads is never False in real play — the vocabulary spoke a condition the admission gate discards. Your answer did not widen the gate: “no LOS means no observation, but a LOS a moment ago means it’s near where it was” — so the lookup moves, memory carries the rest, and the next step is a measurement rather than a design. Two more capabilities landed beside it: the 9K113 sight’s perception half, and emitters — a burning or firing unit now gives itself away out to the 10 km bubble, the first perception path where the target’s behaviour decides whether it is seen. The board’s own prediction last cycle was wrong and worth naming: BL-14 was drawn as blocked on a resolution mechanism nobody had built. It shipped without one, because you removed the requirement — “I can myself approximate attacking a group by picking an individual unit there.” Still unheard in the air: 24 entries carry #needs-flight, and one of them now asks for mountains.

What the drawing shows that the lists do not. A flight is still the hub — but it is no longer the same flight. Last cycle every chain hung off one undifferentiated sortie. Now there are two blocked nodes in the attack-run chain and they are not interchangeable: the queue of 24 merged-but-unheard entries, and a sortie that has to be flown over mountains. Both 2026-10-15 flights were over flat desert, which is exactly where the “obstructed” register barely fires — your own read was “terrain blocked LOS can happen if in mountainous region, airframe blocking is more common.” A repeat of the same ground would measure the same nothing. That is a property of where you fly, which no list on this page had ever had to express before.

The project chose to measure instead of to build, and the drawing is where that choice is visible. BL-B76's first step is not a fix: it widens a log so the two cases that currently look identical — the target was blocked versus no verdict arrived — can be told apart. Your direction was explicit: “before acting on LOS none, we should know how often that happens. As it stands now, log it and then ignore in cockpit behaviour.” The instrument built for that measurement had a one-line defect that made it blind to the exact case it existed to count: its loop skipped every candidate whose gate outcome was not ADMITTED, and a blocked target is by definition not admitted. An instrument that reports the thing it cannot see as absent is worse than no instrument.

The emitter chain is the first group here where the target acts. Everything else in the perception group is a function of the observer — range, optic, gaze, occlusion. A burning hull and a muzzle flash are properties of the thing being looked at, and they deliberately bypass the range and canopy gates while clamping to presence only: a flash at 9 km says something is shooting there, never that is a BMP-2. It closes a gap the pilot meets immediately — a unit firing from 4 km was invisible, because that is past the naked eye's ~2.3 km presence threshold for a 7 m vehicle. The thing most worth telling you about was the hardest for Petrovich to see. The remaining member is the one with a genuinely new shape: a SAM launch's smoke outlives its own event, so it is the first perception source with a decaying external lifetime — where the launcher was, which belief already models, and must never read as where a launcher is now.

And one prediction on this board was simply wrong. Built Built, one slice deep

The double-line edges are DCS itself — read and written, never a one-way feed. The single plain edge between Body Layer and World Model is the one in-process import this project allows anywhere; every other subproject boundary, including the ones crossing back to Windows, is HTTP. Mission Interpreter runs offline and hands off one artifact before a sortie starts — it has no seat in the runtime loop. Note which way the brain's two edges point: body-layer hands an escalation over and does not wait, and the reply is drained in the ordinary poll loop. That is not an implementation detail — it is the constraint the whole layer was designed around, because the game world keeps moving whether or not Petrovich has finished thinking. Only the aircraft layer is pinned to Windows — it needs the DCS install. Body layer and world model sit on the Mac by decision, not by need, and they move together or not at all. The change this cycle is a third box inside Windows: a production firing-events Hook, with per-unit life riding the existing telemetry loop. It matters architecturally because it is the first channel where DCS reports an event rather than answering a query — everything else on that bridge is polled state, and an event that is missed is simply gone, which is why the receiver side carries a latch and a watchdog rather than trusting the feed.
