1 · Heading & turn

Do
  • "Turn left two zero." then "turn right four five." (the one from last flight, unchanged)
  • "Turn left thirty." (number word, not digits)
  • "Turn lift two zero." (deliberately misheard "left" as "lift")
  • "Heading three one five." (the absolute form — new this pass)
Expect

All four work. "Turn lift two zero" still turns left — the near-miss wording tolerance is for the directive word, never the number. "Heading three one five" turns to compass heading 315° directly, not relative to current heading.

Record

Did "turn lift" actually resolve to left, or did it say "say again"? Any delay or odd phrasing on any of the four?

2 · Level flight & level off

Do

Mid-turn or mid-climb, say "level off." Separately, say "level flight."

Expect

"Level off" stops the turn and holds the current heading. "Level flight" freezes heading, altitude and speed together at whatever is currently held. Saying "straight" does not work any more — that wording was dropped (it collided with the hover command "right"); if you say it out of habit, expect "say again," not a readback.

Record

Does dropping "straight" feel like a real loss in practice, now that you've flown without it once?

3 · Speed

Do
  • "Speed two hundred."
  • "Speed max."
  • "Slow down." then "speed up."
Expect

"Speed two hundred" sets 200 (your usual speed unit). "Speed max" commands the AI's own maximum, not a number. "Slow down"/"speed up" step from whatever speed is currently held, one step at a time.

Record

Did "speed max" read back distinctly from a numeric speed command, so you could tell which one happened?

4 · Altitude

Do
  • "Altitude three hundred."
  • "Altitude three zero zero barometric." (digit-by-digit, with the reference word)
  • "Climb fifty." then "descend one hundred."
  • "Hold altitude."
Expect

The readback always names the reference it's actually going to use (radar vs. the 700 m barometric cap), which may not be the one you said — that's existing behaviour, not new. "Climb"/"descend" step relative to the currently held altitude. "Hold altitude" freezes at the current value and never names a reference in its readback.

Record

Did the readback's named reference ever surprise you?

5 · HVRT bare words

Do

In hover mode, say each of: "left", "right", "forward", "back", "hold", "up", "down" — one at a time, pausing between each.

Expect

Every one behaves exactly as before — these are now rules, but matched the same way they always were (an exact bare-word shortcut, not the fuzzy grammar). No audible difference from flying this same set of words before BL-17.

Record

Any of the seven behave differently than you remember?

6 · Hold-and-fly right after taking the controls

Do

From the operator seat, press "Request Aircraft Control" (the AI-overlay handover; a key press, not a phrase), and immediately (within a second or two) say a flight command, e.g. "turn left two zero."

Expect

The command is held briefly, then flown once the takeover settles — not refused, and not silently dropped. You should not need to repeat it.

Record

Did it fly the held command, or did you have to say it again? How long did the hold feel like?

7 · A flight command while not flying

Do

Before taking the controls (or after giving them back), say "turn left thirty."

Expect

"Unable, not flying." — not "say again," and not a readback as if it had been flown.

Record

Was the wording clear that the command was understood but refused, versus not understood at all?

8 · "You have controls" / "I have controls" in AI-overlay mode

Do

Say "you have controls." Then say "I have controls."

Expect

Silence, both times. In AI-overlay mode these phrases are a no-op by design — the actual handover is toggling the AI-overlay flight mode yourself, which this rule does not do. This is not a bug if nothing happens.

Record

Did the silence feel wrong, or did you expect it going in?

9 · A flight readback cutting off a routine callout

Do

While flying and Petrovich is mid-sentence on a routine callout (a contact report, a scan update — anything ordinary, not urgent), say a flight command, e.g. "speed two hundred."

Expect

The flight command's own readback cuts the routine callout off mid-sentence rather than waiting for it to finish. This is a real, deliberate behaviour change from before this pass — flight readbacks now jump the queue while he's flying.

Record

Did the interruption feel abrupt or jarring, or did it read as the right priority? This is exactly the kind of judgment only you flying it can make.

★The one thing that matters most

Step 9's interruption behaviour is new and was built on your own direction, but nobody has heard it happen in a real cockpit yet. Does a flight readback cutting off a routine callout feel right, or too abrupt? This is the one judgment call this whole batch cannot resolve without you in the seat.
