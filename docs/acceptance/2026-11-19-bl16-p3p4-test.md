## Blocks

### Block 1 — Align / align-for-launch, no recovery trigger

Say **"align"**. Expect: the nose swings to the 9K113's azimuth and holds it, no further words
repeated ("Aligning." said once, not on a loop). Say a heading command (e.g. "turn heading 3 1
5"). Expect: it is flown at once — the heading command interrupts the alignment and nothing says
"Aligning." again afterward.

Then, at a safe height, say **"align for launch"**. Expect: the aircraft dives. Confirm no
recovery fires while well above the AGL floor and below the IAS ceiling — it should just hold the
dive attitude, not auto-level, until a recovery trigger actually fires (see Block 2).

### Block 2 — Each recovery trigger independently — **this is the prize block**

This is the one that proves the missile stays guidable through a real pull-out, not just that the
recovery logic fires in isolation.

1. Align for launch and let it cross the AGL-relative drop floor **without** firing a missile.
   Expect: "Leveling off.", altitude restored before speed (watch the AI-pilot-wheel overlay's own
   ALT/SPD boxes — altitude's box should settle first), and heading untouched (it holds whatever
   FLT adopted at the moment recovery started — it is **not** restored to the pre-align heading).
2. Repeat, letting it cross the IAS ceiling instead of the AGL floor. Same expectations.
3. **Repeat with an actual missile launch.** Expect: the missile stays guidable through the
   pull-out — the sight is worked by the player throughout, Petrovich never touches it. This is
   the one case that cannot be faked by a fixture: it needs a real launch, a real recovery firing
   mid-guidance, and a real missile impact or miss to call it good or bad.

**While watching Block 1's dive and this block's recovery, also watch for whether the aircraft
visibly "levels off" (pulls to level flight) before it starts climbing back, or whether it just
climbs directly from the dive attitude.** This is a judgment call the Reviewer flagged rather than
silently settled (`review-p3p4.md`'s Q0 discussion): the code freezes **heading** at its current
value on recovery but jumps altitude/speed straight to the pre-align targets, rather than a literal
two-step "level, then climb." If it looks wrong in the air — a visible lurch, or the aircraft
diving further before recovering — that mismatch between the user's words ("first level at
current, then start recovery") and what got built is where to look first.

### Block 3 — The two overrides

Mid-dive (after "align for launch"), say **"level flight"** or **"level off"**. Expect: behaves
exactly like a recovery (same leveling behaviour as Block 2).

Mid-dive, say **"altitude 2 0 0"**. Expect: the dive aborts and the climb target is 200 m — not
back to whatever altitude was held before "align for launch" was said.

### Block 4 — Breaks

From level flight with a heading goal outstanding, say **"break turn left"**, then separately
**"break turn right"**, then separately **"break away"**. Expect each time: the turn starts
immediately, is not cut short partway, and the old heading goal does not reassert itself
afterward.

Repeat from inside "align" or "align for launch" — say a break command while aligning. Expect: the
break cancels the alignment outright, with no recovery sequence following it (it should not try to
"level off" after a break interrupts an alignment).
