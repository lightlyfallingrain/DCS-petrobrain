Elevation Cost Probe

One question the log can answer, and one only you can. The hook runs itself ten seconds after mission start — your job is to notice whether the aircraft stutters while it does.

Do not combine this with the group-reporting sortie

The probe may hitch the frame by design. That is exactly what would contaminate a judgement about whether Petrovich's callouts feel right — and a cockpit full of callouts is exactly what would mask a hitch. Two flights, not one.

Nothing to check out

The Hook script is already on the Windows box, deployed by the Windows-box session. Its source sits on investigate/terrain-elevation-source, unpushed — so nothing about it is readable from the Mac repo, and every claim on this card about what it does is relayed from that session rather than verified here.

What this flight settles

Bridge cost is already known for getVelocity: 0.5 ms fixed plus ~2.6 µs per item, p99 of 8 ms at 568 units, read out of a sortie already sitting in dcs.log. Two things that figure cannot tell us.

Does land.getHeight work through the bridge at all? High confidence, never demonstrated — every prior elevation probe ran offline through mission-editor triggers.

What does it cost, and does it block DCS's own frame? A different engine call, and the 20 ms outliers in the existing data hint that a payload might stall the renderer rather than merely taking time.

Setup — DCS alone

No collector, no body-layer, no brain, no audio adapter. The hook writes straight to dcs.log.

  1. Start DCS on the Windows box.
  2. Load any Syria mission — the probe's eight reference coordinates are Syria-specific.
  3. Let it run. The hook fires ~10 s after mission start on its own.

Unverified — trigger timing, Syria dependency and output format are all as relayed

Blocks

Block A

Did it return real heights?

Do

Nothing. The hook checks land.getHeight against 8 coordinates whose true heights are already on record.

Expect

Eight comparisons in the log, matching the recorded values. A mismatch is as interesting as a match — it would mean the bridge reaches the function but returns a different surface from the one the earlier probes read.

Record
Block B · the prize

Did the aircraft stutter?

Do

Fly normally through the first minute, and pay attention at the ~10 s mark and the few seconds after. The hook times batches of 1, 100, 500 and 2601 points against a zero-work null call, 20 repeats each.

Expect

Extrapolating from getVelocity, a 2601-point batch is ~7 ms of bridge time — under one frame at 60 fps. If that holds, you should feel nothing at all.

Record — in your own words

A clean “felt nothing” is a real result, not a non-answer — it is what clears live probing to be designed around.
