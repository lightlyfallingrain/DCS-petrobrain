Sortie Refinements Sortie

Three of the four things you asked for after the 2026-10-05 flight: describe as a synonym for report, watch/follow tagging a whole group, and scan ahead sweeping 11-12-1 instead of staring at 12. Item 1, the location-fragment rewrite, is not in this and is still its own branch.

Before anything — do not start a command with "Petrovich"

A transmission that begins with the wake word is routed to the brain as free speech. One without it is matched against the command vocabulary. So say "Describe.", not "Petrovich, describe."

Checked by running the matcher, not by reading it: "Petrovich, describe." comes back with no token at all, while "describe" matches report_all at 1.00.

Setup

The usual script. Nothing on this branch changes it.

./run-scripts/run-crew-text.sh

Unverified — needs the Windows box and a live DCS session, so it could not be run from here. What was checked: every flag it passes (--crew-text, --f10-commands, --speech-audio, --speech-input, --speech-log) still exists and still parses on this branch.

That third line is the exact phrase you spoke at t_sim 258 last flight and that fell through unrecognised. Note it resolves to the clock report rather than the all-contacts one — correct and deliberately more specific, the same way "report eleven o'clock" already behaved. Not a miss.

2 Watch / follow now tags the whole group

Two ways in, and the F10 one is the better in-flight test — it takes speech recognition out of the loop, so a failure means the feature failed rather than the recogniser.

Do

F10 → Petrovich → Watch → Nearest (menu labels read off the shipped hook script), with the nearest contact part of a real multi-vehicle group. Or say "Watch nearest.", "Follow group.", "Follow truck.", "Follow armor three o'clock."

Expect

The readback changes shape entirely — a count, not a description:

two-member group→"Watching two."
three→"Watching three."
four→"Watching four."
above twelve→"Watching many."
single, ungrouped→the old wording — "Watching T-72, 12 o'clock, 0.5 kilometres…"

Those strings are the renderer's real output, run directly.

Two things that look like bugs and are not

The count is what he actually marked, not the group's size. If the group lost members since its last reconcile, fewer get marked and he says the smaller number — and if only one is left, he falls back to the single-contact wording. A group of four reading back "Watching three." is correct. This is the fix the security review asked for: the line used to speak a count it had never checked.

The tag is a one-time snapshot, by your own direction — "Tag once, static." A unit joining that group after the command is not watched; one that leaves stays watched. A late arrival going unreported is the design.

Record

Does the bare count tell you enough, or do you want to hear what you are watching? It deliberately names only the number — naming four vehicle types would make this the longest readback in the vocabulary for the simplest thing you can ask.

3 "Scan ahead" sweeps 11 → 12 → 1

Do

F10 → Petrovich → Scan → Ahead, or say "Scan ahead." / "Look ahead."

Expect

It used to be a fixed stare at 12. It now steps one clock hour at a time — 11, 12, 1, 11, … — which is your own constraint: "still one clock hour at a time. A sweeping scan like any other scan, repeating loop 11-12-1 o'clock."

Read off the code: legs (11, 12, 1), 2.0 s dwell each, so each hour is revisited every 6 s. The cone itself is unchanged at ±15° — not a widened cone, and the DCS line-of-sight query cone is untouched.

Record

The honest cost: 12 o'clock is now dwelled on a third as much, so something dead ahead may be called a beat later than before. Does it feel like scanning, or like dithering? The dwell/revisit interval is an open question, not a settled number — if 6 s is wrong, say what would feel right.

The one worth the most

Does a watched group ever lose a whole kind of report? (BL-B41)

This is a known, filed gap that shipped knowingly, and it is the one thing on this branch that could read as a bug. Its real frequency in flight is unknown — and that number is what decides whether the fix is urgent or can wait.

The mechanism, briefly: one member of a watched group is elected to speak for it, and the others' reports of that kind are dropped. The election is per contact; the dropping is per event. So when the elected member happens to have no event of that kind on that tick, that kind goes unreported for the whole group.

What it looks like

A watched convoy starts moving and you hear nothing about movement. That is BL-B41, not a new defect.

The part that makes it hard to score

The observable is not silence. In the reproduction, the group's own line still fired for an unrelated reason. So "the group said something" is not evidence the movement report survived. What is lost is that kind's content, not every utterance about the group.

What you are unlikely to lose

Range crossings self-correct a poll or two later. And where weapon envelopes are known the widest-envelope member — the most dangerous one — is always the elected speaker, so engagement calls are mostly safe. The exposed case is a partial movement transition inside a tightly-clustered group.

Record

Watch a group, then get it moving, and say whether you heard a movement call. Even one clean observation either way is worth more than everything else on this card — the fix is a real redesign (a per-event election, shared with the merged observability gate's matching gap) and nobody knows yet whether it is annoying or invisible.

One thing to listen for that is not a feature

A commanded scan ahead now changes gaze direction on ~66% of polls instead of ~1%, because the direction cycles instead of sitting still. Per-poll CPU is unchanged and each push is sub-millisecond locally, ~5-10 ms over the LAN — but the aircraft-layer HTTP timeout is 2.0 s with no connection reuse, so that tail is now reachable ~66× more often while a commanded scan ahead is running.

If you get a periodic ~2 s stall in reporting, specifically during a commanded scan ahead, that is the network tail, not the sweep. Measured over loopback only; the LAN figure is unknown. One line in the debrief is enough — it strengthens an already-owned item to shorten those timeouts.
